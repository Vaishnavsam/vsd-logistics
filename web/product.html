SCOPE · THE PROTOTYPE TODAY

A focused foundation,
not a promise of everything.

VSD Logistics is an early, self-hosted operations prototype. Its scope is intentionally clear so the next product decisions can come from real use.

INCLUDED
  • Operator login and shipment lifecycle
  • Driver roster, dispatch, and capacity checks
  • Optional manually entered map coordinates
  • Consent-based driver GPS during transit
  • Customer-safe tracking links with revocation
  • Reusable workflow checklists and event history
  • SQLite persistence and idempotent writes
NOT INCLUDED
  • Geocoding or address validation
  • Road routing, traffic, or ETA prediction
  • Payments, billing, or finance
  • Customer accounts or multi-tenant organizations
  • Automated driver dispatch or optimization
  • Native mobile apps or background GPS service
  • Production hosting and managed support

Map tiles are loaded directly from OpenStreetMap and attribution is shown in the app. The app does not send addresses to a geocoding or routing provider.

01What makes VSD Logistics different?＋

We won’t claim that no other logistics product offers these capabilities. VSD’s point of difference in this prototype is its deliberately connected, self-hostable workflow: shipment operations, consent-based driver location, reusable handoff checklists, and a restricted customer link in one small system.

WHY IT MATTERSLess stitching tools together. Clear boundaries about what the system knows.
02Can my team use it in production today?＋

It’s a functional prototype, not a production-ready logistics platform. The built-in server binds to loopback. Before remote or operational use, you would need a properly secured HTTPS reverse proxy, deployment and backup procedures, monitoring, access policies, and a broader security review.

THE HONEST BOUNDARYTry the workflow locally; do not expose the built-in server directly to the network.
03What does the map show, and what doesn’t it?＋

Operators provide labels and optional coordinates, then can adjust map pins by dragging. The line is a straight-line preview. There is no address verification, geocoding, road routing, traffic data, or ETA calculation. OpenStreetMap tiles load directly with attribution; tile requests reach the tile host when the map is viewed.

NO FALSE PRECISIONPlanned coordinates and verified GPS are shown as different kinds of information.
04When is a driver’s location shared?＋

The driver opens the dedicated page, enters an operator-issued token, and grants browser location permission. The service accepts fixes only while the driver has a shipment in transit. Browser backgrounding may pause updates. A fix older than two minutes is not shown as current on the customer page.

DRIVER CONTROLLocation sharing requires an explicit action and can be stopped from the driver page.
View the driver experience →
05What can a customer see through their link?＋

Only one shipment’s public reference, pickup and drop-off labels, customer-safe status updates, and a recent in-transit GPS fix when available. Cargo, driver identity, internal event metadata, and checklist tasks are excluded. The operator can rotate or revoke the link.

SHARED BY DESIGNA customer gets a useful shipment view, not access to the operations console.
06Where does the prototype keep its data?＋

Operational records are stored in a local SQLite database on the host running the service. Protect the host and database file, choose a unique operator password, and establish backups before treating the data as important.

YOU OWN THE HOSTSelf-hosting gives control, and makes deployment, access, and backups your responsibility.
07What is deliberately outside the project scope?＋

Payments, billing, finance, customer accounts, automated dispatch optimization, native mobile applications, address services, road routing, and ETA prediction are not included. The next product step should come from testing this core loop and learning which operational handoff needs more depth.

FOCUSED FIRST RELEASEBuild around a proven workflow, rather than promising a feature list the prototype does not deliver.
